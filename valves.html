Control fluid flow

A flexible sleeve is at the heart of the valve.

A pinch valve is built around a cylindrical sleeve reinforced with natural or synthetic rubber inside a valve body. In its normal open state, media passes through the sleeve along a direct, full-bore path.

To close the valve, pressure is applied between the valve body and sleeve—typically using compressed air—pinching the sleeve shut. The result is a tight closure without a conventional obstruction in the flow path.

A straightforward principle

Open for flow.
Pinched for shutoff.

The flexible sleeve isolates the process media from the valve’s operating mechanism. This simple arrangement is well suited to fluids that can challenge conventional valve designs.

  1. In the open state, the sleeve provides a clear path for the process media.
  2. Closing pressure acts around the sleeve, compressing it across the flow path.
  3. The pinched sleeve creates a tight shutoff; release the pressure to reopen.
Why pinch valves

Designed for demanding flow paths.

Pinch valve operation offers practical benefits when a process involves wet, abrasive, viscous or paste-like media.

Direct, full-bore flow

A straight-through path helps avoid flow obstruction and loss of head.

Tight shutoff

When pinched closed, the sleeve provides bubble-free tightness, including with wet media.

Handles challenging media

Viscous, paste-like and abrasive materials can pass without choking or blocking the valve.

Fewer moving parts

A simple operating principle means low maintenance requirements and fewer parts in contact with process media.

Self-cleaning sleeve

The sleeve helps resist jamming and seizing when the valve is used with difficult materials.

Flexible application range

Available in body materials including cast iron, aluminum and PVC for different installation needs.
